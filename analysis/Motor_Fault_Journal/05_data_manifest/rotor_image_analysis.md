# Rotor Drilling Image Analysis

## One-hole stage

The photographs show one circular radial opening on the rotor's cylindrical surface.

## Two-hole stage

Two circular openings are clearly visible. They are separated mainly along the rotor's axial direction and lie at a similar projected circumferential height. Because the cage bars are skewed and hidden beneath the laminated surface, this view does not prove whether the two openings intersect one bar or two different bars.

## Three-hole stage

Three surface openings can be identified across the available views. They occur at different axial and projected circumferential locations. Perspective, rotor rotation between photographs, and the absence of an exposed end-ring/bar map prevent reliable counting of the underlying cage bars between holes.

## Defensible conclusion

The images verify progressive **one-hole, two-hole, and three-hole stages** on the same rotor. Image geometry alone does not expose the underlying bars. The experimenter confirms that Hole 1 and Hole 2 targeted adjacent bars, with Hole 2 axially offset downward, while Hole 3 targeted a different bar approximately 180 degrees around the circumference. The manuscript may therefore describe progressive damage to one, two, and three bars, provided this placement is identified as experimenter-confirmed rather than derived solely from the photographs.
